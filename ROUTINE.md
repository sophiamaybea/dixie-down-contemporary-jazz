# The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down — Contemporary + Concert Jazz

**Slug:** dixie-down-contemporary-jazz  
**Song:** The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down — The Band — studio album *The Band* (1969). Not Joan Baez 1971. Not The Last Waltz.  
**Duration:** 3:30–3:32  
**BPM:** published 126 4/4; dancer pulse = 63 BPM half-time (hypothesis ±2). Rehearse marked at 56–58.  
**Meter:** 4/4 album cut  
**Counts:** dancer 8s on the slow pulse (one 8 = 7.62 s = 4 published 126-BPM bars)  
**Eighths:** straight. Jazz delay is an attack on &, not 3-a-4  
**Body vs feet:** half-time dirge in the weight; straight eighths for isolations  
**Axis:** concert/theatrical jazz primary, contemporary secondary. Jazz owns choruses; contemporary owns verses, spiral, floor, and the hole before the chorus downbeat  
**Footwear:** jazz shoes (socks ok for Verse 3 floor)  
**Cast:** solo, Sophia frame 5’9” long line  
**Space:** sprung studio  

Clock times below are architecture at 63 BPM. Slide ±2 s against your exact file. First audible harmonic downbeat = count 1 of 8-count 1.

## Motifs (only these return)
- **A — TORN TRACK** — parallel walk on a DS–US rail; the walk breaks at the standing hip
- **B — BELL** — isolated rib or skull as clapper on the chorus backbeat
- **C — MUD** — yield-push plié that keeps sinking after the beat; full floor only in Verse 3
- **D — NA-LINE** — compact jazz walk, delayed shoulder, eyes off the travel
